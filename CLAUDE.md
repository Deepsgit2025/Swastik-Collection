# CLAUDE.md

## Project
Windows desktop billing app replacing Vyapar for a retail shop. Electron + React + TypeScript + SQLite (better-sqlite3). No server, no cloud DB — fully local-first, with Google Drive used only for scheduled backup snapshots (added in Phase 12).

## Roles (enforced everywhere, permanently)
- **Owner**: full access to everything. Since 2026-09-19 every *screen* is open to the counter; edits and deletes ask for the Owner password (see "Changes of 2026-09-19" below).
- **Salesman**: can create a sale, record Payment-In, quick-add a customer (name+phone only) mid-sale, generate/print barcode labels, log expenses (add only), view their own day's sales total. Cannot edit or delete ANY record anywhere in the app — sales, payments, parties, expenses. This is enforced in the data-access layer, not just hidden in the UI, so a modified frontend cannot bypass it. Any new write operation added in any future phase must go through the same permission-checking wrapper established in Phase 2.

## Explicitly out of scope, permanently
- No purchase module.
- No inventory/stock quantity tracking of any kind.
- No GST/tax fields anywhere.

## Business rules that shape the architecture
- Every **Sale** is sent via WhatsApp to both the customer and the Owner. **This no longer blocks printing** (changed 2026-08-24, client decision — it did until then). A resyncing WhatsApp session was stopping the counter: WhatsApp re-delivers its entire offline backlog before it will send anything, which takes minutes, and for all of those minutes a customer stood waiting for a bill that could not be printed, with an Owner override needed on every single sale. The send is now **queued rather than awaited** — `retryPendingBills` (`electron/whatsapp/send.ts`) re-sends anything outstanding the moment the session reconnects, with nobody having to act. **What this gives up:** printing no longer proves the Owner received their copy, so it is no longer an audit control. What replaces it is visibility — the undelivered count beside the WhatsApp status badge (`countSalesAwaitingWhatsApp`), which links to the Owner's bill-delivery list — plus the automatic retry that makes an undelivered bill temporary rather than permanent. The Owner override still exists but means something different now: **stop retrying this bill**, the escape valve for a wrong or unregistered number that would otherwise retry forever and hold the count above zero forever. It still records who decided and when. `assertSalePrintable` still enforces the `sale:print` permission server-side; only the delivery condition was removed.
- **Payment-In is NOT WhatsApp-gated** — record and done, no print gate.
- **Discount is a single bill-level field** (`sales.total_discount`), applied once to the sale's subtotal — not a per-line-item field. This supersedes the original Phase 5 spec's "compulsory per-line discount column" decision; `sale_items` has no `discount` column as of migration `004_bill_level_discount.sql`. A line's total is always just `quantity * unit_price`.
- Sales support **split payment** — part Cash + part UPI on a single sale.
- Salesman is allowed to override item price at time of sale.
- **Bill retention (changed 2026-09-19, client decision — was 30/60/90)**: a **cash bill** lives in local SQLite for 30 days, then exists only in the Google Drive backup for a further 30 days, then is permanently, irreversibly deleted (no snapshot kept) — **60 days total** (`RETENTION_LOCAL_DAYS = 30`, `RETENTION_TOTAL_DAYS = 60`, `SNAPSHOT_RETENTION_DAYS` derived = 30, in `src/lib/retention.ts`). A **credit bill** is retained for as long as its party owes anything; once the party's balance is *exactly zero* (`ROUND(balance, 2) = 0` — a party holding an advance is never touched) it follows the same clock counted from the party's **last activity** (newest credit bill or payment): 30 days locally, then Drive only, gone **60 days after it was cleared**. Credit bills are removed party by party, **together with that party's payments** (a party at zero has paid what it owed, so removing both keeps the ledger consistent; the party row and balance stay). Borrowing again resets the clock. Nothing is ever locally purged unless a *successful* Google Drive backup newer than the bill (cash) or than the party's last activity (credit) already exists — a bill past its last day that was never backed up is kept and shown in red, never deleted. Retention still ships **off**. Implemented in `src/db/retention.ts` (`findSettledCreditDueForLocalPurge`, `findSettledCreditHeldBack`, `purgeSettledCreditParty`) and wired in `electron/retention/service.ts`.

## Hardware targets (real models, confirmed)
- Label/receipt printer: Posiflow ShipPro 4-inch (USB/Bluetooth thermal).
- Barcode scanner: Helett HT410 — wired USB, keyboard-wedge/HID, plug-and-play, no driver, configurable suffix (Enter/Tab after scan).

## Tech conventions
- TypeScript everywhere, strict mode on.
- Tailwind for styling, no CSS-in-JS.
- better-sqlite3 for all DB access — synchronous API, no need for async/await on queries.
- All DB writes go through a permission-checking wrapper (`assertPermission`, built in Phase 2 at `/src/db/permissions.ts`) keyed on the logged-in user's role — established in Phase 2, must be reused (never bypassed with a direct query) in every later phase.
- **IPC identity comes from a main-process session, never a renderer-supplied argument.** `electron/session.ts` (added in Phase 3) holds a `Map<senderId, SafeUser>`, set only from a verified `auth:login` response inside `electron/main.ts` and cleared on `auth:logout`/window-close. Any `ipcMain.handle` for a role-gated write (or any read that shouldn't work pre-login) must call `requireSession(event)` — which looks the caller up by `event.sender.id` — to get the `SafeUser` it passes into `assertPermission`/the write function, and must NOT accept a `user`/`role` value as a plain IPC argument. Passing `user` as an argument (as the initial Phase 3 Parties write handlers briefly did) lets a modified/patched renderer claim any role it wants, since `contextBridge`-exposed methods only pass along whatever the calling JS supplies — the main process is the only side that can authenticate who's really connected. `preload.ts`'s renderer-facing methods for these actions take no `user` parameter for the same reason. Every future phase's write channels (sales, payments, expenses, settings, backup, user management, ...) must follow this pattern from the start, not just Parties.
- **The session survives an app restart; only Logout ends it (client request, 2026-08-26).** "Once logged in the system should never log out — until they press Logout they stay logged in": the shop's counter machine was starting every morning at the login form, because the session lived only in `electron/session.ts`'s in-memory map and React state and both die with the process. It now also lives in `userData/session.json` (`electron/rememberedSession.ts`), written on a successful `auth:login` and deleted on `auth:logout` — following `backup/tokenStore.ts` exactly: same directory as `app.db` and `drive-auth.json`, mode 0600, **never crosses IPC**. The file holds a *pointer to a row* (`userId` + `username` + `savedAt`), no password and no hash; anyone who can read it can already read `app.db` beside it. **This does not weaken the convention above** — it is deliberately not `localStorage` or anything else the renderer owns. At launch the renderer calls `restoreSession()`, which takes **no arguments**, and the new `auth:restore` handler resolves the identity from that main-process-only file and then re-reads the `users` row via `findActiveUser` (`electron/auth.ts`) before calling `setSession`. So the role still comes from today's database, never from the renderer and never from what was written down at login: a Salesman the Owner has since disabled in Settings is refused at the next launch, and the remembered file is dropped rather than left to fail forever. The `username` is checked against the row the `userId` resolves to for one specific reason — Phase 12's Drive restore overwrites `app.db` wholesale, and ids in an older snapshot need not mean what they mean now; a mismatch must not silently resume a Salesman as the Owner. Renderer side, `AuthProvider` exposes `restoring` and `App`'s `SessionGate` renders nothing until that round trip answers — without it `RequireRole` fires a `Navigate to="/login" replace` before the answer arrives and a remembered session flashes the login form. `LoginScreen` redirects an already-resumed session to `/dashboard`. **Accepted trade-off, stated plainly**: whoever opens the app on that machine is whoever last used it, with that person's role, until someone presses Logout — including an Owner session with Reports, Settings and user management. That is what was asked for on a single shared shop machine; if the Owner ever wants to step away from the counter, Logout is the answer, and it is the only one. Covered by five cases in `App.test.tsx` ("Remembered session") and five in `electron/auth.test.ts` (`findActiveUser`).
- Migrations live in `/src/db/migrations` as numbered `.sql` files, applied in order, tracked in a `_migrations` table, idempotent on every app launch.
- **Never call `window.print()` from the renderer — printing goes through the main process.** Electron does not ship Chromium's print-preview UI, so a renderer-side `window.print()` opens a dead window reading *"This app doesn't support print preview"* and never reaches a printer. Both print paths use `webContents.print({ silent: false, printBackground: true }, cb)` via `printWebContents` in `electron/main.ts`, reached from the renderer as `printSale(saleId)` (the bill — gate-checked, prints, then sets `printed`) or `printPage()` (Phase 4's barcode labels — ungated). What actually prints is still decided by the `@media print` rules in `src/index.css`: only the mounted `.print-sheet` is visible. This bit both print paths — Phase 4's labels and Phase 7's bill were each written with `window.print()` and neither could ever have printed; it went unnoticed because both were "code-complete, hardware-unverified."
- **Dev launch on this machine needs `ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE` cleared.** VS Code (and therefore any terminal or agent running inside it) exports `ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE=1`, which makes the Electron binary behave as a plain Node interpreter — `require('electron')` returns undefined, so `main.ts` dies at `app.whenReady()` with *"Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'whenReady')"* and no window ever appears. Launch with `env -u ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE npm run dev`. Earlier notes in this file called this a sandbox limitation ("`npm run dev` fell back to plain Node") — that diagnosis was wrong; this is the actual cause, and it is fixable.
- **whatsapp-web.js uses the system-installed Chrome, not Puppeteer's own managed download.** `electron/whatsapp/client.ts` passes `puppeteer: { executablePath: 'C:\\Program Files\\Google\\Chrome\\Application\\chrome.exe' }` to the `Client` constructor, and `app/.npmrc` sets `puppeteer_skip_download=true`. Reason: Puppeteer's own Chrome-for-Testing download was unreliably dropping `chrome.exe` on extraction on the dev machine, even with antivirus exclusions added — switching to the system Chrome sidestepped that instead of continuing to chase it. **Known implication**: this is a hardcoded path with no fallback/detection, so any machine running the app must have Chrome installed normally at that default location — the app does not verify, install, or locate Chrome itself. Treat this as a setup requirement for every machine (dev or client) this app runs on, and revisit before Phase 6 ships more broadly (see Phase 6 status below).

## Changes of 2026-09-19 (client request) — read before touching access control
- **The Owner lock on screens is gone; the Owner password now stands in front of every edit and delete.** Dashboard, Parties (and the ledger), Sales, Reports, Connect WhatsApp and Settings all open with no prompt (`RequireOwner` and the sidebar padlock are deleted). What asks for the password is the *action*: `useOwnerGate()` (`src/auth/useOwnerGate.tsx`) shows `OwnerPasswordPrompt` in a dialog when Save/Delete/Restore/etc. is clicked, runs the action while the window is elevated, and calls `lock()` in `finally`. It is wired into: party edit (at *Save Changes*) and delete (after the existing confirmation), label-size save, every Settings write (shop details, Drive credentials/connect/disconnect/schedule/Backup Now, restore, retention enable/run, salesman add/rename/retire, Owner-password change), and WhatsApp connect / log out / re-link the embedded view. Adding a party stays open (`party:create` always was). **Still not the security boundary**: the main-process session role and `assertPermission` are — every write keeps its Owner-only Action, so a patched renderer that skips the dialog still gets `PermissionError`.
- **Reads were opened in `PERMISSIONS` so the open screens can load**: `report:view_all`, `whatsapp:view_deliveries`, `whatsapp:resend_bill` now allow both roles (the name `report:view_all` is kept so every reporting function's first-line assert is unchanged), plus two new read Actions, `backup:view` and `retention:view` (used by `getBackupStatus`, `listRestorePoints`, `previewRetention`, `getPurgeHistory`). Permission harness: 52 checks (26 actions x 2 roles). Tests that pinned "the counter is rejected" for reports were rewritten to pin the new rule, and `reports.test.ts` gained a block asserting every write Action is still Owner-only.
- **Sale screen salesman is typed, not picked.** `NewSaleScreen`'s Salesman `<select>` is now a text input with a `<datalist>` of active salesmen. `createSale` takes `salesmanName` (wins over `salesmanId`): matched case-insensitively, **created if new** (`resolveSalesmanForSale`, `src/db/salesmen.ts`), blank = Unassigned, and a name the Owner switched off is *refused* rather than silently reactivated. This is a deliberate, narrow carve-out from `salesman:manage`: only creating a name as a side effect of a sale is open to the counter; rename and retire stay behind the password. A spelling typo creates a second salesman (Owner can retire it in Settings).
- **The WhatsApp bill image shows the discount.** `buildBillImageHtml` adds a Subtotal / Discount block above the total banner when `total_discount > 0`, so both the Owner's and the customer's copy show it. `send.ts` was not touched.
- **Embedded WhatsApp Web (`WhatsAppWebHost.tsx`) no longer sits on a "Loading WhatsApp Web…" cover.** Our own opaque cover (same wording as WhatsApp's splash) is now shown only during a cold start, capped at 15s, and no longer re-raised by `did-start-loading`; `reload()` is safe before `dom-ready`; the `online` handler only reloads a failed or never-ready view; crashes back off (1s→30s) and ignore `clean-exit`; the parked view sits on-screen at opacity 0 rather than at `left: -20000`; `backgroundThrottling=no`; `allowpopups` is passed as a string (React 19 dropped the boolean); the guest's WebContents also gets the plain-Chrome user agent. **Not live-verified** — needs a real window. Bill *sending* (`electron/whatsapp/*`, `electron/agent/*`) was not touched.

    - **The day-90 Drive deletion needed a design decision the spec did not anticipate, and this is the important entry here.** Section 3 says day 90 "deletes the corresponding data from the Google Drive backup via the Drive API", as though a sale were an addressable Drive object. It is not — Prompt 2 uploads whole-database snapshots, and any one snapshot holds many bills with different expiry dates, so deleting one bill from one would mean rewriting a backup. It turns out not to be necessary, because **the arithmetic closes on its own**: a bill created at `c` is purged locally at `c+30`, after which no *newer* snapshot contains it, so the last snapshot holding it is the one taken at `c+30`, which must therefore die at `(c+30)+60`. **A snapshot taken at T is deleted at T+60.** Checking the other end: a snapshot at T holds bills created between `T−30` and `T`; the oldest needs Drive until `(T−30)+90 = T+60` (exact) and the newest until `T+90`, which it gets from the later snapshots it also appears in (exact). Both ends land precisely and nothing is ever rewritten. `SNAPSHOT_RETENTION_DAYS` is written as `RETENTION_TOTAL_DAYS - RETENTION_LOCAL_DAYS` rather than as a literal `60`, and a test pins that equality — a later reader "correcting" it to 90 to match the headline rule would silently keep every bill on Drive a month past its deletion date. **Confirmed with the client 2026-08-26** alongside two consequences worth knowing: disaster recovery only reaches back 60 days (the client's own "nothing kept past 90 days" rule showing its teeth, and credit sales stay fully recoverable since they are in every current snapshot), and if the safeguard holds a bill back the equivalence stretches — **late, not early**, which is the safe direction.
    - **The safeguard is one `EXISTS` clause, and two details carry its whole weight** (`src/db/retention.ts`): `status = 'success'` (Prompt 2 writes that only once Drive returned a file id, so it means the bytes landed rather than that an attempt was made) and `b.created_at > s.created_at` (a backup taken *before* a sale cannot contain it). Dropping either would delete bills on the strength of a snapshot that never held them. Both have their own test naming the mistake they prevent, and the fixture is built so recent *failed* backups postdate every sale — an implementation that forgot `status = 'success'` would suddenly consider everything backed up and purge the lot.
